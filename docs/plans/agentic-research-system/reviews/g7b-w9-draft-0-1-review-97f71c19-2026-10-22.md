The draft's boundary instincts are sound: observation is not adoption, `dual_owned` is invalid, no legacy Task is
reopened, field-family authority is preserved and owner decisions are surfaced. But its central mechanism, "W9 uses
W11 §7.5 unchanged for every source kind" (`W9:279`), cannot be implemented. W11 is not an unaccepted draft that W9
can extend. Its exact revision is owner-accepted (D-G6-4 limb 1, 2026-08-01). Its strict schema catalogue is

`closed_reference` item with no portfolio target. Several required W9 contents are also missing or only gestured
at: the pilot selection rubric, the bus deprecation path, measurable baselines, and a usable rollback for
transitioned papers.

The deepest single fact found is a record defect, not a W9 defect. W11's owner acceptance (D-G6-4 limb 1) sits on

## 8. Practicality

The draft is proportionate in shape: per-item, one way, few new objects. The overhead risk is in the details
above. A W11-grade source inventory with an independent reproducer for every decision entry, tracker row and
result file is very costly for `closed_reference` items, which change no successor authority. A lighter
closed-reference observation (handle-bound bytes, hash, signed W0 class, no mapping DAG) for items that never
become `successor_owned` would cut the cost substantially. The full W11 chain could then be kept for paper
roots and any item that feeds a successor object. 04-plan §7 already names that risk as a stop rule ("operational
overhead causes routine bypass").
