# §4.5 Regime Stickiness, Conditional Escape, and Family-of-Origin Geometry

Overlapping 10-year career-phase windows identify a small conditional-escape population inside the wider regime typology. In the descriptive window assignment, 7,453 individuals begin in one of the two disadvantaged regimes, R2 (Inactive Low) or R6 (Low-Income Churn), and 416 ever move into an advantaged destination window, an observed escape rate of 5.58%. The corresponding working-age descriptive rate is higher, 386 escapes among 2,163 disadvantaged starters (17.85%), which is why the regression analysis below treats conditional escape as a restricted, window-based outcome rather than as the full-sample regime prevalence contrast used in the first descriptive pass.

Those descriptive rates are also vulnerable to panel continuity. The inverse-probability weighting model was built from verified longitudinal weights rather than the unavailable `lwtresp` variable, using `{wave}_indinub_lw` for UKHLS wave c onward, BHPS longitudinal alternatives where available, and `lw_base = 1` for BHPS wave ba. The propensity model has AUC = 0.7137 and an effective sample size of 57,035 after p1/p99 trimming. The raw maximum weight is 535.8943, reduced to 42.7808 after trimming, with the coefficient of variation falling from 1.3623 to 0.9942. A structural-eligibility sensitivity shows that 25,372 of 27,280 analytical cases (93.01%) come from respondents who could in principle satisfy the 10-year continuity rule, but it does not remove the denominator limitation: permanent attritors remain unobserved. Under Manski-style attrition bounds, the observed 5.58% overall escape rate has a full-pessimism lower bound of 0.44% and a partial-pessimism lower bound of 1.34%.

The modelling sequence therefore separates three questions. Tier 1 is a broad complete-case escape contrast over the full analytical regime typology, where parental NS-SEC is associated with lower odds of being outside R2/R6. Tier 2 restricts the target to first-window R2/R6 starters and asks who subsequently exits that disadvantaged starting position. The design-based IPW/MICE headline then repeats the Tier 2 fixed-effect structure with survey-style clustering and normalised IPW, because weighted household random-effect variance could not be estimated reliably.

The substantive pattern is stable across the defensible conditional-escape models. Starting in R6 rather than R2 is the dominant predictor of later escape: the unweighted Tier 2 household model gives OR = 21.2916, and the IPW/MICE design-based headline gives OR = 34.8691. Parental NS-SEC has the expected broad-sample association in Tier 1, but after conditioning on first-window disadvantaged regime, the M and L parental-origin coefficients are small and statistically weak in the IPW/MICE headline. This should not be read as evidence that family origin is unimportant. The parental-class cross-tabulation over first-window R2/R6 starters is dense and balanced (H/M/L by R2/R6 counts: 1,435/462, 2,049/651, and 2,050/628; n = 7,275; no sparse cells), and it describes parental class as upstream of initial regime placement. Conditioning on initial regime therefore estimates a direct pathway from class origin to escape within disadvantaged starters, not the total family-origin effect on the trajectory regime in which a respondent begins. Formal mediation, in the Imai-Keele-Tingley or Baron-Kenny sense, is deferred to follow-up work.

The household component is deliberately modest. The unweighted conditional GLMM estimates household clustering at ICC = 0.0595 in the complete-case Tier 2 frame, and the unweighted diagnostic companion estimates ICC = 0.0622 on the IPW/MICE model frame. By contrast, the weighted household random-effect route is not a substantive finding: the weighted `glmmTMB` diagnostic diverged to sigma_u = 35.6066 and ICC = 0.9974, WeMix failed a bounded diagnostic with a negative-semi-definite matrix, and a full single-imputation WeMix smoke did not complete after 4,001.4 seconds. The headline is consequently the design-based `svyglm` result, not a weighted household-ICC result.

### §4.5.1 Family-of-Origin Clustering in Local Persistence Features

A separate family-of-origin topology analysis asks whether siblings or same-FOO respondents occupy unusually similar local trajectory geometry. On the full 27,280-person analytical sample, the FOO clustering file contains 18,538 observed FOO clusters and 13,122 within-cluster unordered sibling pairs. In the primary topology-native arm, local persistence features reject the constrained-shuffle null (p = 0.0001999600), with observed mean within-pair distance 0.1132442 and an effect-size ratio of 0.8243. The three reported feature-level ICCs are all strictly above zero: max $H_0$ persistence ICC = 0.5810 [0.5711, 0.5905], total $H_1$ persistence ICC = 0.5096 [0.4961, 0.5222], and the first persistence-landscape integral ICC = 0.4728 [0.4522, 0.4925].

This supports a family-of-origin signature in trajectory geometry, but not a topology-specific signature. The registered comparator analysis reaches the same rejection threshold for raw 90-dimensional bigram coordinates (p = 0.0001999600; effect-size ratio = 0.6604; 90/90 ICC intervals above zero) and for 10-dimensional occupancy/change-count summaries (p = 0.0001999600; effect-size ratio = 0.6547; 10/10 ICC intervals above zero). The locked interpretation is therefore SUPPORT plus SIGNAL_NOT_TOPOLOGY_SPECIFIC: local persistent-homology summaries give a topology-native route to detecting family-coherent career geometry, while the comparator arms show that the coherence is broader trajectory structure rather than something uniquely visible only to persistence.
